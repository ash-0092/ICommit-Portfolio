A baseline is just: **run the metric once, before you try to change anything, and write down the number and the date.**

### Worked example — Promise Keepers

Say you launch on **1 March**. Your metric needs a 4-week window, so the earliest you can read it is **29 March**.

On that date you look at everyone who was active in the last month and count how many fulfilled a shared commitment in at least 2 of the last 4 weeks:

| User   | W1  | W2  | W3  | W4  | Weeks with a fulfilled shared commitment | Promise Keeper? |
| ------ | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Ahmed  | ✓   | ✓   | —   | ✓   | 3                                        | ✅               |
| Sara   | ✓   | —   | —   | —   | 1                                        | ❌               |
| Omar   | —   | —   | ✓   | ✓   | 2                                        | ✅               |
| Nour   | —   | —   | —   | —   | 0                                        | ❌               |
| Khaled | ✓   | —   | ✓   | —   | 2                                        | ✅               |
3 of 5 qualify. Scale that up: if you had 500 monthly active users and 90 of them qualified —

### That's your baseline
Promise Keepers
Baseline: 90 (18% of 500 MAU)
Measured: 29 March 2027
Definition: >=2 of last 4 weeks  `locked 15 Feb 2027` 

Three things recorded: **the number**, **the date**, and **the definition it was measured under**. The third one matters — if you later change the threshold to 3 of 4 weeks, this baseline is void and you take a new one.

### Why it was worth doing

Before the baseline, "get to 300 Promise Keepers by June" was a number with no meaning. After it, you know you're asking for a 3.3× increase in three months — and you can have a real conversation about whether that's achievable or fantasy.

It also means that in June, when someone claims the new onboarding worked, the claim is checkable.

### One bonus worth knowing

You don't always have to wait for launch. You can baseline the **status quo** instead — measure how people perform _today, without your product_.

In your discovery interviews, ask each father: over the last month, how many shared commitments did you and your partner agree on, and how many actually got done on time? If it comes back at roughly 4 in 10, that's your status-quo baseline — and your target stops being an invented number and becomes _"beat WhatsApp and memory."_

That's a much stronger thing to put in front of a stakeholder than a percentage you picked because it sounded ambitious.
